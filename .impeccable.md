## Design Context

### Users

**Primary audience**: Procurement and commercial buyers at OEMs and manufacturing plants — people evaluating industrial chillers, temperature control units, and custom cooling solutions for production lines (CNC machining, laser systems, extrusion, blow molding, welding, tooling).

These are not casual browsers. They arrive with a specification, a budget, and a comparison shortlist. They care about credibility, technical completeness, and speed-to-answer. A decision here can be a six-figure capital purchase tied to a production line, so trust and authority matter as much as feature depth.

**Context of use**: Desktop-first (spec sheets open in tabs), often during focused evaluation sessions. Mobile is secondary but must not be amputated — buyers forward links to colleagues on phones, and plant engineers reference the site in the field.

**Jobs to be done**:
1. Establish that Pro ITECH is a serious, engineering-grade manufacturer — not a reseller or light-industrial vendor.
2. Navigate quickly to the right product series (MINI / MIDI / MAXI / MASTER) and the right application family.
3. Initiate contact: request a quote, schedule a technical consultation, or download specs.

### Brand Personality

**Three-word brand**: engineered, kinetic, authoritative.

**Emotional goal**: The interface should feel like a precision instrument — something *made* rather than assembled. A procurement buyer should come away thinking "these people take thermal dynamics seriously" within the first scroll.

**Voice**: Direct, technically literate, understated. No hyperbole, no marketing fluff. Numbers over adjectives. When motion happens, it should feel like the *physics* of the product showing itself — airflow, pressure gradients, thermal transfer — not decoration.

**Reference gravity**: Rolls-Royce, Siemens Energy. Heavyweight engineering brands where the design carries the weight of the product. Authority through restraint, depth through detail.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Creative north star**: "Kinetic Precision". The interface mirrors the high-stakes world of industrial thermal dynamics — not the static, dated "industrial" aesthetic of heavy borders and cluttered tables, but the invisible-yet-mathematically-exact flow of air and liquid. Intentional asymmetry, technical imagery bleeding off the canvas, and layered tonal depth make this feel like a digital tool as engineered as the product itself.

**Tone**: Restrained kinetic. Motion is used surgically on high-impact moments (hero reveal, section transitions, data visualizations, product configurators) — never as constant decoration. The page should feel alive in the way a chiller unit under load feels alive: you sense the work happening, but the surface stays composed.

**Theme**: Light-primary, with deep navy as a second surface for high-impact "authority" moments (hero, product theater, stats sections). This is derived from use context — procurement buyers read spec-heavy pages in office light, not on a trading floor. Dark is reserved for moments where we're *showing*, not *explaining*.

**Palette** — the full OKLCH scale is encoded in `src/app/globals.css` and is the single source of truth. Rules that apply when using it:
- Core brand: navy primary (`oklch(0.219 0.085 262)`, ≈ former `#001b46`) → primary-container (`oklch(0.316 0.095 262)`, ≈ former `#163061`). Vibrant red secondary (`oklch(0.483 0.205 29)`, ≈ former `#bc0100`) as sparing accent only.
- OKLCH chosen for perceptual uniformity. Chroma reduces toward the extremes of the lightness axis.
- Neutrals are tinted toward the brand hue (chroma ~0.005–0.02) so surfaces feel related to the brand, not generic gray.
- Red is reserved for genuine signal: active cooling indicators, critical CTAs, error states. Never decorative.
- Hero backgrounds use the `.hero-gradient` recipe — `linear-gradient(135deg, var(--primary-deep) 0%, var(--primary-container) 100%)`. The signature navy-to-navy gradient adds depth without tipping into "SaaS blue glow" territory.
- No AI palette tells — no cyan-on-dark, no purple-to-blue gradients, no neon glows.

**Typography** — shipped pairing, loaded via `next/font/google` in `src/app/layout.tsx`:
- **Display**: **Bricolage Grotesque** (variable, weights 400–800). Precision-engineered grotesk with real mechanical character and a fluid optical-size axis. Used on `h1–h6`, `.display-*`, `.title-*`, and the variable-width medallion wordmark (`'wdth' 95`). Letter-spacing ladder: `-0.02em` at display-lg, tightening to `-0.005em` at title-lg.
- **Body**: **Host Grotesk**. Neutral but uncommon workhorse with engineering feel and proper tabular numerals for spec tables. OpenType features enabled on `body`: `"ss01", "cv11", "tnum"`.
- **Tabular numerals** — use `.tabular` or `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on every capacity, temperature, and stat number so spec columns align.
- **Banned** (reflex-reject list + hard bans): Syne, Space Grotesk, Inter, IBM Plex, DM Sans, Outfit, Plus Jakarta, Instrument Sans, Fraunces, Newsreader, Lora, Crimson, Playfair, Cormorant, and every font on the impeccable skill's reflex-reject list.
- **Pairing rule**: one distinctive display, one refined body. No monospace posing as "technical."

**Layout & depth**:
- **No-Line Rule.** No decorative 1px borders, no side-stripe accents, no card-inside-card nesting. Depth through tonal layering and generous asymmetric space. Break the grid intentionally on editorial moments; stay disciplined on spec-heavy content.
- **Tonal Layering over drop shadows.** Depth is a result of stacking surface tiers, not soft shadows. A `surface-container-low` card sits atop a `surface` section. This mimics the way light hits physical industrial panels.
- **Ambient shadows only when an element must float.** Use the tinted `.shadow-ambient` recipe (`color-mix(in oklch, var(--primary) 8%, transparent)`). Never pure black, never harsh.
- **Ghost Border fallback.** If a border is required for accessibility, use `--border` (15% opacity tinted toward brand hue). Felt, not seen.
- **Cards & lists have no divider lines.** Vertical whitespace from the spacing scale does the separation. Hover state uses a background tint, never a stroke.
- **Sharp radius only.** `0.125rem` (`rounded-sm`) for everything. No `rounded-full` except for status chips and dot indicators. The design is architectural, not bubbly.
- **Section padding** clamps 5.5–7rem (`.section-pad`) so content breathes on wide screens without collapsing on narrow ones.

**Photography**: Macro industrial photography only — cooling fins, compressors, hydronic modules, steel surfaces, refrigerant flow. Zero stock photos of people in suits. The machines are the heroes.

**Motion language**: Exponential easing (ease-out-quart/quint), never bounce. Motion serves the metaphor of thermal flow — staggered reveals that feel like pressure equalizing, not items popping in. One orchestrated hero moment beats twenty scattered micro-animations.

### Localization

Bilingual: German (DACH primary market) and English (international buyers). Structure content and layouts so German compounds (which run long) never break the design — line lengths and card widths must survive 30%+ text expansion.

### Design Principles

1. **Specificity over flourish.** Every design decision must earn its keep by serving the buyer's evaluation task. If an animation doesn't reinforce "we understand thermal dynamics," cut it. If a layout doesn't help someone compare MIDI vs MAXI faster, restructure it.

2. **Authority through restraint.** This is a Rolls-Royce/Siemens-tier brand, not an experimental studio portfolio. Boldness comes from precision, typographic confidence, and unapologetic use of industrial photography — not from chaos or novelty effects.

3. **Motion as physics, not decoration.** Kinetic moments should feel like the product's own behavior — thermal gradients, airflow, pressure curves. Never generic "AI motion": no random float, no rotating icons, no parallax for its own sake.

4. **Numbers are the hero.** Procurement buyers read capacities, efficiencies, and compatibilities. Tabular numerals, high-contrast data typography, and spec layouts that scan in seconds take priority over hero copy.

5. **No AI design tells.** Zero gradient text, zero side-stripe borders, zero glassmorphism used decoratively (glassmorphism is allowed on the floating nav only, per the existing system), zero purple-to-cyan gradients, zero rounded-icon-above-heading templates, zero handshake stock photos. If a pattern shows up in generic AI output, it does not ship here.

6. **Engineered for German.** Layouts must accommodate long compound words. Test every card, button, and headline in both DE and EN before declaring it done.

### Component conventions

- **Buttons.** Primary variant uses a tonal gradient fill from `var(--primary)` to `var(--primary-container)`, sharp `rounded-sm`, `title-sm` label. Secondary uses solid `var(--secondary)` for critical signal only. Tertiary is bare text with a 2px underline that expands on hover.
- **Inputs.** Floating labels. Resting state: `var(--input)` background with no visible border. Focus state: transition to `var(--surface)` with a 1px ghost ring at `var(--ring)` (25% primary).
- **Cards & lists.** No divider strokes. Vertical whitespace from the spacing scale separates items. Hover uses a `surface-container` tint, never a border.
- **Data visualization.** When the product's behavior has to be *shown* — a precision gauge, an active cooling indicator, a temperature ramp — the red accent is the needle / progress channel against a primary-deep track. This is the one place red is allowed as a prominent graphic element.
